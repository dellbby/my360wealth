Who We Help

  • Families whose wealth is spread across businesses, property, entities and investment accounts
  • Families on the other side of a liquidity event who now have more to coordinate than to invest
  • Families where one person holds all the knowledge and nobody else could step in
  • Families supporting adult children, ageing parents, or both at once
  • Families who have good advisers who have never spoken to each other

What the Relationship Covers

  • One point of contact coordinating your attorney, CPA, insurance and banking relationships around a single plan
  • The whole balance sheet — operating businesses, real estate, entities, private holdings and public markets
  • Estate strategy coordinated with your attorney, with titling and beneficiaries kept current
  • Multi-year tax planning across entities and generations, with your CPA
  • Family governance, next-generation education and structured family meetings
  • A document vault and complete balance sheet your family can reach
  • A written continuity protocol, so nothing depends on one person being available

What We Don’t Do

  • We don’t replace your attorney or your CPA — we coordinate with them
  • We don’t charge a percentage that grows every year you succeed; the Family Office relationship is a flat, capped annual fee
  • We don’t require every asset to move to us before we’ll coordinate it
  • We don’t take over decisions — the family decides, we make sure the decision is informed

Frequently Asked Questions

What does a Family CFO actually do?

The same function a CFO performs for a company, applied to a family: holds the complete picture, coordinates the specialists, keeps the reporting current, and makes sure decisions are made with the whole balance sheet in view.

How is the fee structured?

Family Office relationships are engaged on one flat annual fee, capped, rather than a percentage of assets. Our other services are priced differently. Your actual fee is set out in your advisory agreement and Form ADV Part 2A.

Do we have to move our accounts?

No. Coordination does not require custody. Where consolidating makes sense we will say so, and where it doesn’t we won’t.

What happens to my family if something happens to me?

That is the point of the continuity protocol. The balance sheet, the documents and the adviser relationships already sit in one place your family can reach, so they continue with people who know the situation.
